# Microbit Missions

## Small board. Big ideas.

What could you make with two buttons, a display and a few lines of code? In this unit, you will turn a BBC micro:bit into a pocket character, a dice, a treasure alarm and a wireless messaging system.

You will learn programming concepts using **Microsoft MakeCode Blocks**, with micro:bit examples that show how each idea works. Your teacher will guide you through the concept pages and the matching tasks in your workbook. You will predict what your code will do, try it in the simulator, test it on a real micro:bit and improve it using evidence from your tests.

Your final mission brings these skills together: **design, build and improve a classroom help system** that lets a pupil request help and a teacher acknowledge the request.

## Learn a concept, then apply it

Start with the [Teaching lessons](concepts/index.md) section for explanations, diagrams and worked examples. Each numbered teaching page links the concept to a micro:bit example and a workbook activity. Revisit earlier programs as you learn new ideas. The practical reference guides retain their original project numbers.

Sequence, selection and iteration are the three core constructs. The other concept pages explain the data, events and communication that help you build complete programs.

## What you will learn

By the end of the unit, you will be able to:

- Explain how **inputs, processing and outputs** work together in a physical computing system.
- Use **events, sequences and loops** to control what a program does and when it runs.
- Store information in **variables** and use **selection** to make decisions.
- Work with sensor readings, random numbers, coordinates and ordered sequences.
- Send and receive **radio messages** between two micro:bits.
- Use **functions** to organise and reuse code.
- Test a program against its requirements, find faults and explain your improvements.


## Before you begin

You will need:

- A computer with access to [Microsoft MakeCode for micro:bit](https://makecode.microbit.org/){ target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" }.
- A BBC micro:bit and a **USB data cable** for downloading and testing your programs.
- Your workbook to record designs, code screenshots, test results and explanations.
- A partner and **two powered micro:bits** for the radio missions.

Use the **Blocks** editor throughout the unit. Some missions use a starter program or framework; follow your teacher's instructions to access it.

!!! tip "Build, test, improve"
    Predict the result before running your code. Compare what actually happens with what you expected. If something goes wrong, record the fault, make a change and test again.

## Make your progress visible

For every mission, save your project using the name given in the lesson and download a **`.hex` backup**. Add readable screenshots to your workbook, record actual test results and answer the questions in your own words. When working with a partner, both of you should be able to explain how the code works.

Keep your micro:bit close to the desk and handle its cable carefully. For radio tasks, use your teacher's allocated group so your messages reach the intended boards.
